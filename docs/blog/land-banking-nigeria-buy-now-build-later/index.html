Buy the Land Now, Build When You're Ready: How Land Banking Works in Nigeria

Most people think buying land means you must be ready to build immediately. That is not true. In Nigeria, one of the smartest moves a buyer can make is land banking: buy a verified plot in a growing area now, hold it while the area develops, and build later — or sell when the value has grown. It is how many of today's Lekki and Ajah landlords started. They bought when the area was still bush and cheap. The ones who bought in estates, with proper documents, are the ones smiling now.

What land banking really is

You buy land, you secure it, and you wait. While you wait, three things push the value up: new roads, new jobs nearby, and new neighbours building around you. You are not betting on luck — you are buying ahead of development. A plot that costs ₦1.5 million today in a developing axis can be worth several times that in a few years once the road is tarred, the estate fills up, and demand arrives. That is the whole idea.

Why it works in Nigeria

Housing deficit — Nigeria needs millions of homes, and demand for land near cities keeps rising.

Infrastructure follows — new expressways, refineries, free trade zones and airports pull growth in one direction, and land along that direction appreciates.

Land does not expire — unlike a car or a phone, a well-titled plot does not lose value from use. It sits, and the area grows around it.

Our own estates sit on this logic. Maven Estate Phase 3 in Epe offers 300 sqm at ₦1.5 million and 500 sqm at ₦2.5 million; Key Haven Estate in Imota offers 300 sqm at ₦1.74 million and 500 sqm at ₦2.9 million. These are entry prices in areas where roads and development are visibly advancing.

How to bank land the right way

  1. Buy inside an estate, not bush land. An estate gives you a registered survey, a deed of assignment, roads, fencing, and neighbours. Development follows people — and people follow estates. A lone plot in the bush is a gamble; a plot inside a growing estate is a plan.
  2. Verify the title before you pay. C of O, Government Allocation, Deed of Assignment and Registered Survey — know what backs your plot, and confirm it at the registry or surveyor-general's office.
  3. Think 3–5 years, not 3–5 months. Land banking rewards patience. The biggest gains come to buyers who hold through the road construction, the first houses, and the arrival of neighbours.
  4. Secure the plot. Fence it, or at least mark it clearly with beacons, and keep your documents safe. An unsecured plot invites trouble.
  5. Start small if you must. You do not need a 500 sqm plot to start. A 300 sqm plot gets you in the game sooner — and you can always buy a second plot later.

The risks — and how to avoid them

Buying land with a bad title. This is the number one way people lose money. Verify, verify, verify.

Buying where nothing is happening. If there is no road plan, no estate, no development and no neighbours, your plot may sit for a very long time. Buy where growth is visible.

Paying cash to the wrong person. Always pay into a verifiable company account, get receipts in your own name, and collect your allocation letter.

One more thing

Land banking is not just for the rich. You do not need tens of millions. A 300 sqm plot in Epe starts under ₦2 million total, and many estates offer payment plans — for example, Key Haven offers a 6-month interest-free window. If you can save a deposit, you can start. The buyers who regret land banking are almost always the ones who waited too long, not the ones who started small.

The bottom line

Buy the land now, build when you're ready. Verify the title, buy inside a growing estate, secure the plot, and give it time. That is land banking — simple, patient, and proven.
