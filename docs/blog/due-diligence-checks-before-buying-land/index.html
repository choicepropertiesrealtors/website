7 Due-Diligence Checks Before You Pay for Any Land in Nigeria

Every land fraud story in Nigeria starts the same way: "the price was too good" and "we didn't verify." Genuine sellers expect verification — it is the fraudsters who rush you. Run these seven checks before a single naira leaves your account.

1. Search the title at the land registry

Take the document (C of O number, deed, survey plan) to the state land registry and run a search. You're confirming the seller is who they claim to be and the land isn't under government acquisition or dispute.

2. Confirm the survey at the surveyor-general's office

A Registered Survey has a plan number. Verify it lodges correctly and the coordinates match the physical land. This single step kills most "we'll show you one plot and sell you another" scams.

3. Inspect the land physically — with the surveyor

Walk the exact plot with a surveyor, not just the agent. Confirm beacons, boundaries, and that the land matches the survey plan.

4. Ask about excision and gazette (for village/family land)

If the land was ever under government acquisition, ask for proof of excision or gazette releasing it. No excision, no deal.

5. Meet the real owners — and get a receipt trail

For family land, meet the family head and principal members. For companies, verify CAC registration. Payments go to verifiable accounts, never cash to an individual with no receipt.

6. Check for disputes and "omo onile" issues

Ask neighbours, the community, and local agents about disputes. Land with a court case attached is land you don't want.

7. Get everything in writing

Deed of assignment, survey plan, receipts for every payment, and — for estates — your allocation letter. Verbal promises are worthless.
