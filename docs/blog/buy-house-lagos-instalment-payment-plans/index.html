How to Buy a House in Nigeria on Instalments: Payment Plans Explained in Plain Naira

"Can I pay small small?" is the question we hear most on WhatsApp. The answer is yes. Most new estates in Lagos and Ogun now sell on payment plans. But the plans are not all the same, and the difference can be millions of naira. Here is how they work.

1. The initial deposit

Almost every plan starts with an initial deposit. It secures your unit and starts your payment clock. On our current listings, deposits range from ₦5,000,000 (Villa Nova 2-Bedroom, Celebration Parkland, ÈRÍ Lagos) to a percentage of the price, such as 30% at Heirs Residence in Ajah.

2. The interest-free window

Many developers give a short period where you pay the same price as an outright buyer. Key Haven Estate, Kajola, gives 6 months interest-free. Villa Nova Bungalows in Abijo GRA gives 3 months. Heirs Residence lets you spread the balance over 6 months interest-free. If you can finish inside that window, you pay no extra.

3. Tiered prices: the longer you pay, the more you pay

After the free window, the price usually steps up. Real example, Key Haven 2-Bedroom + BQ:

Outright / 0–6 months: ₦59,000,000

7–12 months: ₦66,080,000

13–18 months: ₦73,160,000

19–24 months: ₦80,240,000

Paying over 24 months instead of 6 costs ₦21.24 million more. At ÈRÍ Lagos the 3-Bedroom goes from ₦85m (0–3 months) to ₦91m (12 months). Villa Nova adds 10% for 6 months and 15% for 12 months. Always ask for the full tier table before you commit.

4. Mortgages: the long road

Some estates work with mortgage banks for 10 years or more. Urban Signature in Eleko offers a mortgage through MREIF: 10% equity (₦11,000,000 on the ₦110m bungalow), with the balance over 10 years at 9.75% per year, for buyers under 60. Mortgages give you the longest time, but they come with bank checks, paperwork and interest. Ask what the bank needs before you pay anything.

5. What happens if you miss a payment?

This is the question most buyers forget to ask. Before you sign, get these answers in writing:

Is there a grace period?

Is there a penalty, and how much?

Can the developer cancel your unit, and will you get a refund?

When do you get your keys and documents? On full payment, or before?

Our honest advice

Pick the shortest plan you can truly afford. Every extra month usually costs money. Save up a bigger deposit if you can, and aim to finish inside the interest-free window. Then make every payment into the developer's company account and keep every receipt in your own name.

One more thing: count the "extra" fees too

Some prices do not include documentation, development or infrastructure fees. These can add millions. Read our guide to the real cost of buying land or a house before you budget.
