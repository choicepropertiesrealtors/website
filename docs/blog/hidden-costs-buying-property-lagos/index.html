The Real Cost of Buying Land or a House in Nigeria: Fees Nobody Tells You About

You see a plot for ₦12.5 million and start saving for ₦12.5 million. Then the extra fees arrive. This happens to many first-time buyers. Here are the costs to ask about before you pay a kobo.

1. Documentation fee

This covers your deed of assignment, allocation papers and title processing. Real examples from our listings: Creek View Extension, Mowe, charges ₦3,500,000 documentation on 300 sqm and ₦5,000,000 on 500 sqm. Wells 3 Apartment in Surulere charges ₦900,000.

2. Development or infrastructure fee

This pays for roads, drainage, fencing, the gate and power in the estate. It can be a fixed amount or a percentage. Queen's Home in Mowe charges ₦1,500,000 developmental fee plus ₦750,000 survey and legal. Wells 3 adds 5% for infrastructure and 5% for furnishing. So a ₦65m studio there totals ₦72.4m.

3. Survey fee

Your plot needs a survey plan showing its exact boundaries and beacons. Some estates include it; others charge it separately. Ask.

4. Legal fee

Your own lawyer checks the title and the contract. Lawyers usually charge a percentage of the price. Agree the fee in writing before work starts. It is money well spent.

5. Agency fee

If you buy through an agent, ask upfront whether an agency fee applies and how much. A good agent tells you before you inspect, not after you fall in love with the place.

6. Title perfection

If you need Governor's Consent or C of O after buying, the government charges fees based on the property's value. Ask your lawyer for the current rate in the state where the property is.

7. Building costs (for land buyers)

Some estates set a time limit to start building, and some charge a building approval fee. Fencing your plot and placing beacons also cost money.

Look for "all-inclusive" prices

Some developers now quote one total that already includes the fees. Vision Home Estate and Lekki Picasso City quote all-inclusive plot prices. Villa Nova Bungalows shows its totals clearly: the 2-Bedroom is ₦70m plus ₦7.5m development and documentation, ₦77.5m in all. All-inclusive prices make budgeting easier.

Your checklist before you pay

Ask: "Is this price all-inclusive?"

Get a written breakdown of every fee.

Ask when each fee is due: now, during the plan, or at allocation.

Pay only into a company account and keep every receipt.
