Omo Onile or a Real Estate Company? Who Should You Buy Land From?

Every land buyer in Lagos, Ogun and across the South-West faces this choice. The omo onile (the family or community that owns the land by tradition) says, "Why pay a company more? Buy direct from us, cheaper." The company says, "Buy from us, everything is documented." Both can be true. Here is how to compare them honestly.

Who is the omo onile?

Omo onile means "children of the land". They are the families and communities who own land by tradition. Many of today's estates were first bought from omo onile families. Buying from them is not illegal. The risk is in how it is done.

1. Title and documents

Omo onile: often a family receipt, a deed signed by family heads, and a survey plan. The land may still sit under government acquisition, unless it has been excised and gazetted for that community. If it is under acquisition, the family cannot give you a good title.

Reputable company: the estate usually comes with a stated title: C of O, Governor's Consent, Government Allocation, or a registered survey with deed of assignment. You can check that title at the land registry before you pay.

2. Who actually owns it?

Omo onile: one family can have several branches. If the wrong branch sells to you, or the same plot is sold to two people, you can end up in a dispute that lasts for years.

Company: the company bought the land, so you deal with one seller with an office, a registered name and a company account.

3. Extra levies

Omo onile: buyers commonly face "foundation money", "roofing money" and other demands at each stage of building. Lagos State, for example, passed a property protection law in 2016 that makes forceful entry and illegal levies by land grabbers a crime, but disputes still happen.

Company: a proper estate tells you its fees upfront: documentation, development, survey. No surprise levies at your foundation. (See our guide to the real cost of buying land or a house.)

4. Price

Omo onile: usually cheaper per plot. That is the attraction.

Company: usually costs more, because you pay for roads, fencing, the gate, security, and title processing.

But cheap land is only cheap if you keep it. Losing ₦3 million to a dispute is far more expensive than paying ₦1 million more for a clean, documented plot.

5. Infrastructure and resale

Omo onile: often bush land with no road plan. Development may take years.

Company estate: roads, drainage, fencing and neighbours who are also building. Buyers pay more for plots inside a growing estate, so resale is easier.

6. Payment plans

Omo onile: usually cash, upfront, to individuals.

Company: payment plans are common. Many of our estates offer instalments, some interest-free for the first months.

If you still buy from an omo onile

  1. Search the land at the state Lands Registry or Surveyor-General's office first. Is it under government acquisition? Is it excised?
  2. Confirm the family's head and principal members, and get them all to sign.
  3. Use your own lawyer and surveyor, and visit the land with them.
  4. Never pay cash to individuals. Pay through traceable transfer and get receipts.
  5. Ask neighbours and the community whether the land has any dispute.

Our honest take

If you are experienced, have a good lawyer, and the land is excised and clean, buying from an omo onile can work. For most buyers, especially first-timers and Nigerians abroad, a reputable company estate is the safer road. You pay a little more for a title you can check, fees you know upfront, and peace of mind when you start building.

How to tell a reputable company

It has an office you can visit and a registered business name.

It tells you the exact title before you pay.

It takes payment into a company account and issues receipts.

It lets you inspect the land, and you can see real infrastructure work going on.

It answers your questions in writing.
