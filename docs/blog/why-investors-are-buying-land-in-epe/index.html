Why Smart Investors Are Buying Land in Epe Right Now

Ten years ago, telling someone you bought land in Epe earned you a pitying look. Today, that same land is the smartest conversation at dinner parties. What changed? Infrastructure — and infrastructure is what moves land prices.

The drivers

The Lekki-Epe Expressway expansion has cut travel time and opened the corridor to serious development. The Dangote Refinery and Lekki Free Trade Zone in the Ibeju-Lekki/Epe axis are pulling jobs, workers, and housing demand eastward. The proposed Lekki-Epe International Airport — when it lands, history says nearby land values jump. And government attention is visible: new roads, drainage and estate developments are reshaping the axis.

The price gap is the opportunity

Land in fully built-up Lekki now runs into hundreds of millions per plot. In Epe and its environs — Igbonla, Okenla, Ilara — serviced estate plots still start under ₦2 million for 300 sqm. You are buying tomorrow's Lekki at yesterday's prices. Investors who bought in Ajah fifteen years ago watched the same movie; Epe is the sequel.

How to play it right

  1. Buy inside an estate, not bush land. Estates like our Maven Estate Phase 2 & 3 and Key Haven Phase 1 give you registered survey, deed of assignment, and — critically — neighbours. Development follows people.
  2. Think 3–5 years. Land banking rewards patience. Buy, and let infrastructure do the heavy lifting.
  3. Verify the title. Excision, gazette, C of O, registered survey — know what backs your plot (see our guide to property titles).

The bottom line

Epe is not a gamble; it is arithmetic. Jobs plus roads plus a housing deficit equals demand, and demand equals appreciation. The window where entry prices are this low will not stay open forever.
